Thesis

Identifying Inaccessible Brands and Accessibility Questions from Conversations Between Visually Impaired Individuals and their Sighted Interpreters

Open thesis (PDF) →

Abstract / Summary (editable)

The goal is to understand the challenges faced by visually impaired individuals in their daily lives. To do so, we analyze video conversations between a sighted volunteer and a visually impaired user that include both visual and audio modalities. Two pipelines are developed: a video pipeline that extracts information on brands of products that are difficult for visually impaired individuals to access, and an audio pipeline that captures the conversation between the volunteer and the user to gain insights into the types of inaccessibility issues that visually impaired users face. Various computer vision, audio, and text processing methods are employed for this analysis. Results are analyzed quantitatively using metrics such as precision, recall, and F1 score. We discovered that a combination of the video and audio analysis pipelines outperforms relying on the video pipeline alone. A qualitative analysis of the audio pipeline also revealed that it extracts all questions asked by the user and the corresponding responses of the volunteer in most cases. In closing, this work presents an efficient way to analyze videos of conversations between visually impaired individuals and volunteers, enabling the identification of the specific brands and types of issues that visually impaired individuals face in their daily lives.
