Hi! I’m Ojasvi.

I did my Bachelor’s in Computer Science in India and earned my Master’s in Computer Science from the University of Colorado Boulder. I’m currently a Software Engineer II at Microsoft in the HPC/AI org, where I build systems and automation to validate and optimize large-scale GPU clusters for distributed training workloads.

My interests sit at the intersection of distributed training, computer vision, and NLP—especially building practical, high-performance tooling that makes ML workloads faster, more reliable, and easier to debug.

What I do

Most of my work is ML systems + GPU infrastructure: making large-scale training runs stable, measurable, and fast. Here are the themes that summarize my experience so far:

  • Cluster validation for distributed training: led GB200 cluster validation as team lead + lead developer, building an automated training-based pipeline (including MoE workloads) using rack-aware placement, node/rack bisection isolation, and DCGM telemetry to validate compute + communication fabric.
  • Benchmarking and performance configuration: contributed to MLPerf Training and MLPerf Inference submissions over multiple cycles, collaborating with NVIDIA on optimal configurations (incl. GB300 inference and ongoing GB200 training work).
  • LLM evaluation and reproducibility: built an LLM evaluation pipeline using standardized datasets (SQuAD, MMLU, PIQA), replicating published results to compare implementations and quantization variants.
  • LLM inference optimization: developed C++/CUDA inference for quantized LLaMA models using FlashAttention and cuBLASLt, improving throughput and reducing memory footprint via lower-precision weights.
  • Applied NLP at scale: built embedding + PySpark pipelines to cluster millions of bug reports and improve triage workflows; previously led an information extraction pipeline (CNN + NLP) for procurement documents (~87% accuracy).

Current interests

  • Distributed training: efficiency, stability, debugging, and scaling behaviors (especially communication-heavy workloads).
  • Computer vision & multimodal ML: building practical systems that connect vision/audio/text in real-world settings.
  • NLP: structured extraction, evaluation pipelines, and “systems-first” NLP that works reliably at scale.
